Introduction to Biology

1. What is Biology?
Biology = study of life. From Greek bios = life, logos = study.
A person who studies Biology = Biologist.
Living things are also called organisms - plants, animals, fungi, microorganisms like bacteria and amoeba.
Size ranges from very small (bacteria, cannot see with naked eyes) to very large (trees, birds, hippo, whale).

2. Life & Cell
Life = state of living. Basic unit of life = cell.
Cells are too small to see with naked eyes. We use a microscope to see them. Process = magnification.
Unicellular = made of one cell. E.g. bacteria, amoeba.
Multicellular = made of millions of cells. E.g. human, fish, tree.

3. Branches of Biology

2 Main branches: Botany (plants) + Zoology (animals). Botanist studies plants, Zoologist studies animals.

11 Other branches:
1. Anatomy - Physical structures of organisms
2. Cytology - Structure and functions of cells
3. Ecology - Relationship between organisms and environment
4. Entomology - Insects
5. Genetics - Heredity and variation
6. Immunology - Immune systems
7. Microbiology - Microorganisms
8. Mycology - Fungi
9. Parasitology - Parasites
10. Taxonomy - Classification of organisms
11. Physiology - Physical and chemical processes inside living organisms

4. 7 Characteristics of Living Things

1. Nutrition: Taking food from environment. Plants, blue-green algae and some protoctists make their own food using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. Animals feed on ready-made food - plants, other animals, or microorganisms. Example: Lion eats meat. Food enables living things to grow, develop, and carry out other life processes.

2. Respiration: Food contains energy, but not in directly usable form. Food has to be broken down within living cells to release energy. This process is respiration. Energy is used for growth, movement, and other activities.

3. Excretion: Nutrition + Respiration = Metabolism. Metabolism produces useful substances and by-products. Some by-products are harmful if left in body. Excretion = removing metabolic waste. Examples: urea, excess water, carbon dioxide. Urea + excess water removed as urine and sweat. Water vapour + CO2 removed when we breathe out.

4. Growth and Development: Growth = increase in number of cells, so increase in size and weight. Development = cells become specialised to perform specific functions. Example: Bean seed → plant, Egg → chick → hen.

5. Sensitivity: Detect and respond to changes in environment. Changes = stimuli. Examples: Human feels heat, cold, pain. Snail hides in shell when touched. Mimosa pudica - leaves fold when touched. Plants respond but slower than animals.

6. Movement: All organisms move on their own. Non-living only move when pushed/pulled. Animals move whole body = locomotion - walk, run, crawl. Example: Cheetah running. Plants move parts - roots towards water, shoots towards light.

7. Reproduction: Producing new individuals of same kind. Example: Human gives birth, maize seed grows to maize plant, chick hatches from egg. Ensures continuity. Non-living do NOT reproduce.

5. Importance of Studying Biology

(a) Understand and care for environment
(b) Identify and group living things - makes it easier to study them
(c) Appreciate nature
(d) Understand our bodies better
(e) Get research skills - measuring, observing, analysing, concluding
(f) Improve food production - develop high-yield, disease-resistant varieties = hybrids
(g) Improve standard of living - know causes, symptoms, transmission of diseases
(h) Answer fundamental questions - When did life originate? Without plants, will animals survive?
(i) Prevent, treat, cure diseases. Example: First cure for malaria was quinine from bark of Cinchona tree
(j) Foundation for careers - teaching, nursing, medical, horticulture, environmental science
(k) Encourage international cooperation - biologists cooperate to find cure for COVID-19, AIDS, Ebola

6. Relationship With Other Fields

Biology is NOT isolated. Related to Agriculture, Medicine, Pharmacy, Veterinary Medicine, Nutrition, Forestry.

Agriculture: Growing crops + rearing animals. Biology gives improved breeds that mature quickly, lay eggs early (layers start at 5 months vs indigenous at 8 months), resist diseases and harsh climate. Example: maize varieties resistant to disease.
